## The two limiting active fractions

Set \(b=(c/A)^\omega\). The zero-noise Q1 mass distribution on the scaled input has density
\[
g_b(h)=\frac{A}{2\pi b}\frac1{[1+(h/b)^2]^2},\qquad h>0.
\]
It integrates to \(A/8\), since the limiting label weight is \(A\cos^2\beta\,d\beta/(2\pi)\). Its active fraction at \(\zeta\) is
\[
F_0(\zeta)=\frac4\pi\int_0^{\max(\zeta,0)/b}\frac{dt}{(1+t^2)^2}.
\]

For Gaussian target-only evolution let \(a=\rho K(\rho a)\). On \(w>a\) put
\[
 B(w)=\tfrac12[w-\rho K(\rho w)],\qquad
 H(w)=w\exp\!\left[-\omega\int_w^\infty
        \left(B(r)^{-1}-(\omega r)^{-1}\right)dr\right].
\]
The positive-noise limiting density is \(g_b(H(x))H'(x)\) on \(x>a\). Its active fraction is zero for \(\zeta\le a\), and otherwise
\[
 F_+(\zeta)=\frac4\pi\int_0^{H(\zeta)/b}\frac{dt}{(1+t^2)^2}.
\]
The exact failure in the proposed positive-noise angle law is \(H^{-1}(h)>h\) for every finite \(h>0\). Indeed \(0<B(w)<\omega w\) above the root. The time-of-flight integral therefore has a strictly positive correction even after the outer logarithmic time has been removed. This identifies a leading gate contribution on the critical input scale.

Labelwise estimates hold on fixed compact interior Q1 angle sets. Convergence of masses and fractions holds over the entire initial Q1 sector by cutting off endpoint strips of initial length \(\eta\), each with terminal mass \(O(A\eta)\), and then sending \(\eta\) to zero. Neither endpoint carries an atom. Uniformity includes the full ratio box, compact positive ranges of \(c,A\), and compact offset ranges for the fractions. The gate is tested at the current angle. ## Leading-system result and its limits

At \(N=0\), source 1 alone gives \(x^{[1]}(t)=x_0\sqrt{m_0/M(t)}\). The full input receives a source-2 correction, and the output also receives the source-1 donor term \(-\mathcal Y/2\). With \(D_0=\log[(1-m_0)/m_0]\) and \(R_*=(1+R_0)e^{2D_0}-1\), both are bounded in the saturation lemma by integrating the actual field against the kernel \(\sqrt{M(s)/M(t)}\). No smallness of the displacement is inferred when \(m_0\) is small.

For two ordered labels in a single self-consistent leading field, write \(k=y_2-y_1\). The two off-diagonal receiver derivatives equal \(\lambda\Phi(\rho x)/2>0\). Their order is preserved, and
\[
 (\sqrt M\,k)'=(\rho/2)\sqrt M\,[K(\rho x_2)-K(\rho x_1)]\ge0 .
\]
Thus an initially aligned spread with separated positive-weight quantiles cannot contract to one coherent point under this zero-weak-mass evolution from fixed \(m_0>0\). This argument controls a finite-amplitude spread, including donor backreaction. P spectra independently gives the local behavior at the resident: at \(M=1\) the centered aligned and antisymmetric rates are \(+\alpha_{\rm sh}\) and \(-\alpha_{\rm sh}\), while the coherent mean block is attracting. At \(M=0\) the aligned local rate is \(-d\); \(-\omega\) is the outer zero-noise rate. There is no single positive exponential growth law here for arbitrary finite spreads.

The candidate weak seed \(N_0\asymp S_0^{1-\lambda}\) gives a formal local gain \(N_0^{-\alpha_{\rm sh}/\lambda}\asymp q^{-P}\). This power predicts failure of local smallness before or by weak half mass. It does not measure the nonlinear weak-half-mass profile. A full Q1 comparison limit has unbounded scaled support, whereas the saturation lemma is for a prescribed compact leading entry state.
