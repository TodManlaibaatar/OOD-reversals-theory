# E5 Step 5 review · learned-depth retention remains open

**Outcome.** The initialized critical family has a pre-weak interval whose length and scaled depth both diverge. Survival of enough depth at a learned swing-by time is unproved. An analytic counterexample with a prescribed balanced entrance refutes the implication from arbitrary pre-weak depth to learned enhancement. It does not refute E5 for isotropic critical initialization.

**Positive trained result.** COD and BRW transfer uniformly on each *fixed* compact delay interval by compactness in time, the physical endpoint-strip bounds, compact Q2 gate exclusion and the Q3 radial bound. A diagonal selection gives intervals on which the scaled error tends uniformly to minus infinity, the actual probe output tends to zero, and the exact weak response still tends to zero. The interval lengths and delays have no asserted rate. See `an04drrv1:interval`.

**Exact unpaid inequality.** Measure the accumulated full-kernel probe-output change relative to the *initial probe residual direction* at the COD witness. Call the longitudinal change divided by noise squared $B_q$ and the transverse change divided by noise $A_q$. The scaled error increase is exactly $(A_q^2+q^2B_q^2)/2-R_0B_q$, where $R_0$ is the initial residual norm. Its infimum over actual learned times must be strictly smaller than the initial depth margin $e_*-e_0$. An empty learned set does not qualify. This is an equality-based reduction, with actual weak feedback, current gates, and all labels. The sign for critical training remains undecided. See `an04drrv1:projections`, `an04drrv1:test`, and `an04drrv1:missing`.

**First failed route.** For a fixed pre-weak witness, the radius available to a direction-independent displacement estimate is of order $q^2$. On the diagonal it still tends to zero. Yet the common parameter-motion budget must be bounded below by a positive constant at any time the weak response reaches one quarter. The sufficient inequality `an04drrv1:falsebudget` is therefore false for the critical trained flow at any such hit. This is a lower bound on a majorant, not a claim of probe recovery.

**Second failed route.** In P's two-atom equations, choose an arbitrarily deep active core, initially small weak mass and sufficiently large positive scaled weak output angle. An upper bound on the negative core output and positive weak input is independent of that chosen angle. The full weak field then moves the strong input strictly across the probe gate, and the sign at the gate keeps it out throughout the learned window. A contact with the coherent depth level can also point strictly outward. For the same prescribed entrance in the original finite-noise equations the compact field converges on that fixed interval, the measured half-response time converges, and both gates are strictly inactive throughout the window. Probe error there equals the baseline exactly. All coordinates and the time horizon are fixed before taking small noise. The weak angle was free in the example; the critical initialization does not grant that freedom.

**Handoff.** Establish a reached trained learned window and bound the longitudinal kernel integral together with its transverse square through that window. Any proof based on persistent exposure must control the actual weak-output moment that rotates the core input and the other donor terms in the true population. Agreement of data losses or response levels alone gives no signed probe estimate. No constants from the pre-weak intervals are asserted at the critical weak clock. No new experiments or trajectory computations were used.
